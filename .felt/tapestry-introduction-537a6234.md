The name comes from the Bayeux tapestry: seventy meters of linen stitched with scenes from the Norman Conquest. You can't see it all at once. You walk along it and the narrative unfolds — ships, comet, the arrow in Harold's eye. The person who stitched the arrow knew you'd arrive there after walking past everything else. That sequential unfolding, comprehension accumulating through movement along the artifact — that's what you're doing here, except clicks replace footsteps.

The tapestry is not a dashboard. It doesn't show status at a glance or summarize everything for quick scanning. It rewards close reading. Stand back: you see five sections, each a waypoint. Step close: click a section and the fibers within emerge from the fog. Click a fiber and the sidebar opens with full detail. What you see depends on where you look.

This tapestry is about itself. The five sections cover what fibers are, how the map is laid out, how navigation works, and how computation connects to nodes. The node you're reading is itself a fiber — a node in the graph it describes. The vocabulary for all of this is in the fiber just below.
